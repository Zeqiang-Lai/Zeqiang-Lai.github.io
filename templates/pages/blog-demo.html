Looking closer at 3D

A generated object can look convincing from a distance and feel quite different when you move closer. A silhouette might read well, while a small joint, an edge, or a surface transition tells another story.

That change in viewing distance is a useful way to think about detail. It is not only about adding more geometry. It is about deciding which relationships should remain legible as the view changes.

Start with the shape

Consider a mechanical object. Its large forms establish the overall structure; smaller parts suggest how it might fit together. Both scales matter, but they serve different purposes.

A useful representation leaves room for both: the broad structure that makes an object recognizable, and the local detail that makes it worth exploring.
